### 1. 960×540 @ 30 fps at M2P p95 ≤ 120 ms on 5 GHz Wi-Fi, 3 OSes — needs owner

- **Simulator measurement** ([`reports/latency-2026-09-28-simulator.json`](latency-2026-09-28-simulator.json), `"environment": "simulator"`; method in `docs/SRS.md` §13.7): 960×540 JPEG q80, fps cap 30, 60 s, orbit motion, macOS host over loopback. M2P p50/p95/p99 = **102/120/120 ms** (1 566 frames); verdict `meets: true` "M2P p95 120.0 ms <= 120.0 ms", scoped "simulator only". Two more runs of the same setup gave p95 108 ms (60 s) and 112 ms (15 s).
- **Legs (p50/p95/p99, ms):** pose leg 8.5/16.0/16.8, render/readback 2.2/3.2/9.1, encode 37.8/39.0/39.5, send 0.7/0.8/0.8, network (derived residual) 49.6/40.1/29.2, receive 0.7/1.9/2.1, decode 2.4/5.7/6.1, display 0.9/14.1/17.2. Harness: `tools/latency/run_simulator.sh`, `tools/latency/merge_report.py` (22 pytest cases, 8 of 8 mutations caught).
- **Why it isn't met:**
  - The simulator's display time is `MTLCommandBuffer.GPUEndTime` (the simulator SDK has no `MTLDrawable.presentedTime`), a lower bound that leaves out the wait for the next refresh. On a device the app uses `presentedTime` (`SightlineIOS/SightlineIOS/Viewfinder.swift:312`), which has never run.
  - No Wi-Fi: the simulator and Blender share one Mac and loopback.
  - Only macOS was measured; Windows and Linux hosts weren't.
  - 30 fps wasn't reached. The debug wheel's JPEG encode (p50 37.8 ms) limits the single encoder thread to about 26 fps (1 / 37.8 ms, derived). The HUD showed 17–24 fps in simulator runs. SRS §13.2 (S-2a) measured release encode near 1 ms at 960×540, but a release wheel can't be linked on this Mac (`docs/LOOP_LOG.md`), so release numbers need CI wheels or the owner.
- **What exists for the owner's run:** the device measures M2P as display − capture time of the frame's `pose_seq` (`SightlineIOS/SightlineIOS/DeviceLatency.swift:68`, `:188`), reports the real p95 in `VIDEO_REPORT.m2p_p95_ms` (`TrackingPipeline.swift:532`), which drives the host's adaptive quality (test `native/vcam-py/src/video.rs:532`); the host logs render/readback, encode and send legs (`BlenderAddOn/core/latency.py:174`); the phone writes `latency-device.json` and shows an optional latency overlay.

### 2. Lens and tap-to-focus controls work — met in the simulator only

- **End to end, simulator app → Blender camera → next streamed frame** (full `tools/mission/qa_ios.sh uitest` against the QA host, milestone validation of `lens` and `inputs`; contract assertions VAL-CROSS-001 and VAL-CROSS-005 passed):
  - `testLensControlsDriveTheBlenderCameraAndTheFrame` (`SightlineIOS/SightlineIOSUITests/SightlineUITests.swift:261`): each prime sets Blender's `lens` and changes the streamed picture (mean pixel difference 24→135 mm 46.5, 135→85 mm 10.7, 85→85 mm 0.0); slider to 12 mm, pinch out 42 mm, in 17 mm, max 300 mm; aperture and DoF reach the camera.
  - `testTapToFocusAndRackBetweenMarks` (`:349`): a tap on the totem focuses at 1.3 m, the lower right at 3.4–3.6 m (the floor); a tap in the pillar bar sends nothing; a 2 s A→B rack rises monotonically on the host (from about 1.1–1.3 m to 10.8–11.4 m across runs) and ends at B in `hud.lens`.
  - `testStreamFollowsTheHostRenderAspect` (`:434`), `testLensStateSurvivesAHostRestart` (`:456`), `testHardwareButtonsActOnTheHost` (`:497`, light press = focus at the centre, volume = rack A/B, through the simulator input hook).
- **Host side, headless Blender with the fake iPhone:** `tests/blender/addon_lens.py` (focal, focus, f-stop, DoF, sensor presets, AUTO fit, idempotent resends), `tests/blender/addon_focus.py` (tap distance along the view axis 4.600 m vs 5.008 m euclidean; smoothstep rack 1→6 m in 1.00 s; cancel by manual focus, tap or Blender edit; tap in a 2.39:1 stream). Ray cast on the main thread (`BlenderAddOn/core/apply.py:133`), rack on the timer tick (`:297`).
- **Protocol:** CONTROL_STATE bits 4–9 and the STATUS applied-lens block (`docs/protocol/vcp.md` §6.2/§6.4), golden vectors checked by Rust, Swift and Python; O-4's T2 part is closed (`docs/protocol/vcp.md:539`).
- **Open:** physical touch, pinch and ARKit on an iPhone (owner). DoF and f-stop changes reach the camera, but the default Solid stream doesn't show depth of field, so they don't change the streamed picture.

### 3. Blender UI stays responsive (NFR-PERF-002) — needs owner

NFR-PERF-002: with streaming at default settings on the reference machine, the main thread never blocks for more than 50 ms because of Sightline. Everything below is the **headless** QA host with the debug wheel; no GUI Blender was run in this mission.

- Simulator M2P run, 960×540 Solid, 1 517 frames: per-frame render/readback p50/p95/p99 2.2/3.2/9.1 ms, max 36.9 ms ([`reports/latency-2026-09-28-simulator.json`](latency-2026-09-28-simulator.json)).
- 10-minute soak, 2 % loss and 10 ms jitter, 13 772 frames: render/readback p99 3.2 ms but **max 60.8 ms**; pose apply p99 0.17 ms, max 0.60 ms ([`reports/soak-2026-09-28-macos-arm64.json`](soak-2026-09-28-macos-arm64.json)). Render/readback is the sum of a frame's draw on one tick and its readback on the next, so the 60.8 ms sample doesn't prove a single 60 ms block, but the per-tick maximum isn't recorded separately, so it doesn't rule one out either.
- During a rack and taps, the QA host's time between polls stayed ≤ 9.94 ms, and `tests/blender/addon_focus.py` measured a maximum poll of 0.61 ms (VAL-LENSB-017 passed). JPEG encode and sending run on Rust threads, off the main thread.
- **Needs owner:** a GUI Blender run on the reference machine at default settings (Solid, 960×540, 30 fps) that records the longest main-thread block, ideally with a release wheel, and the same on Windows and Linux GPUs. An earlier 7 s GUI run (iteration 61) had timer p95 26.3 ms.

## Not verified, needs owner (full list)

1. **Real iPhone and ARKit tracking:** the app has only run in the simulator with scripted poses; 1.5.1c (device pose-leg run) and O-1 (which clock `ARFrame.timestamp` uses) are open.
2. **Wi-Fi and Bonjour on a LAN:** pairing, discovery and the device part of NET-004 (reconnect after Wi-Fi roaming or sleep).
3. **On-device M2P:** `presentedTime`-based M2P and every leg on a real iPhone over 5 GHz Wi-Fi, with macOS, Windows and Linux hosts, at 30 fps with a release wheel (gate item 1).
4. **Hardware buttons and capture-event delivery to an ARKit app (FR-CTL-008):**
   - Whether an ARKit-only app receives capture events at all (AVKit header: "The system sends capture events only to apps that actively use the camera").
   - Every press on a device: volume up/down, Action button, Camera Control click and light press, game-controller connect and sticks; and that volume keeps its system behaviour while stopped or with Settings open.
   - **Decision for T3:** `AVCaptureEventInteraction` delivers a Camera Control click to the primary handler exactly like volume down and doesn't identify the button; a light press isn't a capture event at all (it drives the `AVCaptureControl` overlay, which needs an app-owned `AVCaptureSession`). So on a device a Camera Control click racks to A, and the approved "full press = record" and "light press = focus at the centre" mappings are reachable only through injected input events (the simulator QA hook). The owner should choose a T3 mapping, for example a long primary press = record.
5. **60-minute device thermal run (NFR-PERF-001)**, and real thermal-state transitions (gate item 4).
6. **Blender UI responsiveness in a GUI session** on the reference machine and on Windows/Linux GPUs (gate item 3).
7. **Signing, notarization and TestFlight** (XP-004..006; Apple credentials).
8. **The 2-hour CI soak (NFR-REL-003):** dispatch the `soak` job (`workflow_dispatch`, `soak_minutes` 120) once the branch is on GitHub. Untested until then: `tc netem` on the `ubuntu-latest` kernel and the 40 ms pose-leg limit under netem.
9. **README media:** a device recording to replace the simulator `docs/media/viewfinder.gif` (plan M.4), and the owner-filmed M.5/M.6 clips. Optional follow-up: `docs/media/viewfinder.gif` (2.6 MiB) sits on the `tools/check_large_files.py` allowlist under the plan's 3 MB README-GIF cap; allowlist entries could get a byte cap.
